# S3 (Qwen3.5-0.8B Q4_K_M): improved skeleton-of-thought for ONE reply (2026-10-26)

Question: can parallel section expansion (skeleton-of-thought, SoT) make ONE stitched reply faster per word than plain sequential decoding of a reply of similar length, and does quality hold? Plan: [../docs/PLAN-single-reply.md](PLAN-single-reply.md) S3; earlier naive result: `2026-10-26-parallel-subtasks.md`.

## Setup and methodology

- Model `Qwen_Qwen3.5-0.8B-Q4_K_M.gguf` only, llama.cpp b11884 CUDA, `llama-server -ngl 99 -fa on -np {1,4,8} -c 2048*np` (per-slot context 2048 >= 1536), default continuous batching. A fresh server per `-np`; GPU checked idle (0% util, 0 MiB) before each start. No power / clock / graph / thread / system setting was changed. Hardware details: FINDINGS section 1 (RTX 3050 A Laptop 4 GB); the power plan and AC state were not re-recorded in this session.
- Raw `/completion` with the Qwen chat format and an empty think block (as in `scripts/parallel_subtasks.py`), greedy (temperature 0, seed 1), `cache_prompt=false` except in the prefix test. Outline re-rolls (variant b only, max 2 extra) use temperature 0.8 with fixed seeds.
- Per server session: one discarded warm-up (one SoT reply + one sequential reply), then **3 reps** (5 recommended by the plan; 3 accepted here for the time budget) per (config, topic); plain-decode control (256-token reply, 5 reps) before and after every session (drift 0.0-1.0%, table at the end).
- Topics T1-T6: electric cars, vaccines, how a bill becomes a law, photosynthesis, internet packet routing, binary search. Main comparison: all 6 topics at np=4, N=6, L=100. Reduced grid N {4,6,8} x L {50,100,200} on T1-T3 at np=4. `-np` {1,4,8} on T1-T3 for N=6 and 8. Prefix test: T1-T3 only.
- **Variants.** (a) naive: the earlier prompt ("exactly N key points, short phrase"), sections get the whole outline but no neighbour info, no intro/conclusion. (b) distinct: outline prompt demanding N non-overlapping sections with 2-5 word titles plus a worked few-shot example; automatic re-roll (up to 3 attempts) if max pairwise word-set Jaccard overlap between titles > 0.5, then dedup of remaining overlapping titles; each section prompt contains the outline, "section k of N", its previous/next titles and "no introduction / restating / conclusion"; plus a 2-sentence intro and a 2-sentence conclusion generated in parallel with the sections from the outline (the conclusion does not see the section texts). Stitching = concatenation in order.
- **L** is the section request: "about 30/60/120 words" with `n_predict` 70/140/280 tokens (the model does not follow word counts closely; a cut section is trimmed to its last full sentence; truncation counts are in the tables). N is the number of requested sections; "points" is how many survived parsing/dedup.
- **Sequential control** (plain decode, same server): "Write a clear, well-organised explanation of <topic> in about W words" with W = the SoT reply's word count rounded to 25. **The 0.8B does not follow word targets** (sequential replies were 0.7-1.9x the target), so speed is compared **per word** (words / wall seconds) and the matched sequential word count is shown. Some long sequential replies fall into repetition loops (see caveats), which also pollutes the sequential quality baseline.
- **Metrics.** wall = whole reply incl. outline, re-rolls, intro/conclusion and (in prefix test) priming. **Speed-up per word** = (SoT words / SoT wall) / (sequential words / sequential wall), computed per config on means over reps, then averaged over topics. **ideal** = reply tokens / (outline tokens incl. re-rolls + critical path of the expansion jobs on np slots, LPT schedule); "ideal (infinite slots)" uses the longest single job. Hardware-independent. realised/ideal = realised speed-up / ideal. Quality (all **crude**): distinct 2-gram / 4-gram ratio, repeated-sentence rate (sentence pairs with content-word Jaccard >= 0.8), keyword coverage (fixed 8 topic keywords per topic, a substring check), and an "auto rubric" 0-2 = mean of three 0/1/2 bands (coverage, distinct-4gram, repetition). They do not measure factual accuracy, coherence or transitions; the manual read below does, on only 2 topics and by one (non-blind) reader.
- Scripts: `scripts/s3_sot.py` (runner), `scripts/s3_report.py` (tables below). Raw: `2026-10-26-s3-sot-0.8b.raw.json`, stitched sample replies: `2026-10-26-s3-samples.json` (T1, T2 at np=4: each variant + sequential).

## Headline

Ratios are per word vs the same-session sequential control; **[measured]** unless marked.

- Variant (b) at N=6, L=100, np=4: **1.49x per word** (6 topics, range 1.40-1.59; +/- ~0.1 per topic from rep SD), 3.2 s for 548 words vs 5.2 s sequential for 602 words. Best cell of the grid: **1.72x (N=6, L=200, np=4, T1-T3; 889 words)**; best np: np=8, N=6 gives 1.59x (np=4 1.50x on the same T1-T3).
- Naive outline (a): 1.25x (1.09-1.49), consistent with the earlier 1.14-1.25x.
- Realised speed-up is only **0.4-0.55 of the ideal critical-path bound** at np>=4 (aggregate decode throughput on this GPU is ~1.7x one stream and each stream drops to ~0.53x at np=4, ~0.35x at np=8). The knee is np~4: np=8 adds only +0.0-0.1x.
- **Speed criterion (>=1.4x per word) is met at N=6-8, L>=100; quality criterion is not**: auto rubric 1.33 vs 1.72 sequential (77%; the plan asks for within 10%); repeated-sentence rate is lower than sequential on average (0.017 vs 0.074) but higher in 3 of 6 topics; the manual read of T1 found factual nonsense (see below). Short sections (L=50) give only 1.0-1.1x. See conclusions at the end.

## Manual read of the stitched outputs (T1 electric cars, T2 vaccines; np=4, N=6, L=100; full texts in `2026-10-26-s3-samples.json`)

One reader, not blind, small sample; the judgement of "factual error" is from general knowledge.

- **T1, naive (a)** [~290 words]: the outline repeats itself ("battery powers motor", "motor drives wheels", "uses stored energy") and the sections restate "battery -> motor -> wheels" six times with almost no new content; no intro or conclusion; the section on charging says the battery "is charged by sunlight or electricity" and calls the battery pack "the central engine". Coverage poor, repetition high, transitions none. Worse than the sequential reply.
- **T1, distinct (b)** [~440 words]: the outline looks distinct but is **wrong for the topic**: "Engine produces heat", "Exhaust gases cool the engine" (an electric car has neither); the sections follow the outline, so ~2 of 7 sections are factual nonsense and the intro/conclusion repeat it ("the engine generates heat, and exhaust gases cool the engine"). "Electric motors generate electricity" is also misleading. Other sections are fine and not repetitive. The sequential reply (275 words) is generic but mostly correct; it has its own error (an "alternator" in an EV). Verdict: (b) is less repetitive than (a) but **less correct than sequential**; the coherence score of the intro/conclusion is good, section-to-section transitions are absent.
- **T2, naive (a)** [~330 words]: every section says "antibodies neutralise the virus ... memory cells ... future infections"; one section is a single sentence; no coverage of vaccines vs infection beyond the first lines. Highly repetitive (distinct-4gram 0.77, repeated-sentence rate 0.20).
- **T2, distinct (b)** [~600 words]: outline (antigen presentation, immune activation, cytokine release, T cell differentiation, B cell memory, antibody production) is distinct and mostly accurate (APC/MHC, helper vs cytotoxic T cells, memory B cells and plasma cells are correct at a textbook level), more detailed than the sequential reply (325 words, which is accurate but generic). Weaknesses: sections 1 and 2 begin with the identical sentence and cover the same ground; "immune response activation" is not given a section of its own; the conclusion mislabels "T cells differentiate into memory cells"; the intro is a single run-on sentence; no transitions. Verdict: comparable to or better than sequential in depth, with some repetition; the best case.
- Overall: the improved outline prompt reduces repetition and raises depth when the model knows the topic (T2) but can **hallucinate a distinct-looking outline when it does not follow the topic's real structure** (T1); because every section is conditioned on that outline, an outline error propagates to 1-2 sections and to the intro/conclusion. The automatic metrics catch repetition (T2 naive) but not the T1 error, so they overrate (b) there.

## Conclusions and caveats

1. **[measured] Speed.** Improved SoT reaches 1.4-1.6x per word at N=6-8, L=100, np=4-8 and up to 1.72x at L=200 on 3 topics; naive 1.25x. The plan's expectation (1.3-1.8x per word at N=4-6, np=3-4) **[estimate]** is met on speed. Gains rise with section length because the fixed outline/intro costs (~0.6-0.8 s) amortise: 1.0-1.1x at L=50, 1.4-1.5x at L=100, 1.6-1.7x at L=200. More sections beyond 6 do not help (N=8 is 0.1x lower than N=6 at every L; the 8-section outlines also yield more truncated and more repeated sections).
2. **[measured] realised/ideal 0.4-0.55 at np>=4; 0.9 at np=1.** SoT at np=1 is slower than plain (0.83-0.84x per word). The bound `ideal` is not approached because per-stream speed falls to 0.53x at np=4 (aggregate 1.7x): the claim "speed-up follows ideal up to the knee" holds only approximately at np=2-3 and is not supported at np>=4 here; np=8 adds <=0.1x. (The aggregate-decode ratio 1.7x is hardware-specific; `ideal` and the shapes transfer, the knee does not.)
3. **[measured] Quality did not hold under the plan's criterion.** Auto rubric 1.33 vs 1.72 (77%) over 6 topics; keyword coverage 0.67 vs 0.81; distinct-4gram 0.91 vs 0.92; repeated-sentence rate 0.017 vs 0.074 (but 0.03-0.04 vs 0.00 in T4-T6); manual read: T1 factual nonsense from a bad outline, T2 acceptable. The distinct outline prompt did lower outline overlap (max Jaccard 0.51 -> 0.17; re-rolls were needed on 20% of replies) and cut repetition vs naive (0.079 -> 0.017), so "quality depends on outline distinctness" is supported; "no worse than sequential" is not. Plan success criterion (>=1.4x AND rubric within 10% AND no topic with worse repetition) is **not met**; speed alone is.
4. **[measured] Prefix reuse (cache_prompt) did not help.** Priming every slot with the shared prefix (~100-150 tokens) saved 438 of ~1130 prompt tokens per reply but added a serial priming step (+0.13 s) and a slower run: 1.29x vs 1.45x per word on T1-T3 (prefill of such short prompts is negligible vs decode; the 0.16x gap is about 1.5x the per-topic SD, so it is suggestive, not conclusive: the safe reading is no gain). It matters only for much longer shared contexts.
5. Caveats: 3 reps, 6 topics (3 for the grid/np/prefix), one model/quant, greedy, one machine; the sequential control does not follow the word target so ratios are per word and reply lengths differ between cells; the (b) vs (a) comparison changes several things at once (outline prompt, section context, intro/conclusion, resulting reply length), so it is not an ablation of each factor (the plan's separate `distinct` and `distinct + context` rows were dropped to fit the budget); long sequential replies sometimes degenerate into repetition loops (T3 in the naive-matched control, T5 and N=8 / L=200 matched controls: distinct-4gram 0.3-0.7), which lowers the sequential quality baseline in those cells and makes the "within 10%" comparison fairer to SoT in the grid than in the main table; the automatic rubric is crude and the manual read is two topics by one non-blind reader; speed-up ratios are specific to this GPU's batch-scaling curve.
6. Not run: N in {2,3,12}, L=400, np in {2,3,6,16}, separate ablation rows for (b) without context / without intro-conclusion, 5 reps, blind rubric by a second rater, larger models.
